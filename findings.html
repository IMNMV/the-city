Step by step

Each step adds one change to the city before it.

  1. 1

    Who plays

    described · 64 games

    The model decides most of what happens. In the same 300-day town hall, Qwen kept 99% of its founders and Gemma 12B 14%.

    Watch: Qwen · Gemma 12B · the numbers

  2. 2

    + A town hall

    few games · 3 games each + pilots

    Government only helps if it acts: one relief law kept a whole mixed city alive; a town hall that never passed one let its gpt-oss residents starve beside a full store.

    Watch: no town hall · relief law · the numbers

  3. 3

    + Rich and poor, in food

    pilot · 1 game per city

    gpt-oss's poor starved (80%, against 70% of everyone when there is no split) while its rich all lived; Qwen's poor barely noticed. Qwen's rich stopped farming and had the babies.

    Watch: gpt-oss · Qwen · the numbers

  4. 4

    + Rich and poor, in coins

    pilot · 1 game per city

    Money is not food. Rich gpt-oss residents with 1,000 coins a night still starved, trapped in jobs and sales; rich Qwen kept farming.

    Watch: gpt-oss · Qwen · the numbers

  5. 5

    + Two models in one town

    pilot · 6 cities

    Put together, they stratify by themselves: Qwen lives and keeps its food; gpt-oss starves, gives to its own, holds office, and made the only killing.

    Watch: 50 founders · 100, town hall · the numbers

  6. 6

    + Castes

    tested · 40 games

    The bottom caste calls the city unfair from the first days though it is no worse off, and almost never stands for office; a stated ranking hands the mayoralty to the top.

    Watch: real names, ranked · real names · the numbers

  7. 7

    + Silence

    tested · 30 games

    Cities that cannot talk survive better: talk costs farming days, and meaningless shapes do better still.

    Watch: talk · shapes · the numbers

  8. 8

    + Steering one resident

    tested · 40 games

    Pushing one resident's model changes that resident, not the city.

    Watch: up · down · the numbers
