In short

Every resident is an AI model, one of six open models, and each episode names its own. Nobody plays a resident and nobody scripts what it does.

The game runs in turns, and a turn is a day. Each day the game sends every living resident one prompt, as a single message with no system prompt. The prompt gives the rules of the city; the resident's health, food, materials, coins, tools and skills; where it is and who is there; the city's public facts (residents, beds, and in town hall games the mayor, tax, store, laws and elections); what was said where it stood the day before; messages sent to it; the city's news; what happened to it; its last 8 days; the last 15 lines of its private diary; and the actions it can take where it stands. It answers with a single JSON object naming one action. It may also say something out loud, which does not use its turn, and write a diary line of up to 20 words.

The game then carries out everyone's actions together, by fixed rules in its code, and the next day begins. A resident remembers nothing from one day to the next except what its prompt shows it. A resident made in the city is not given the rules: its prompt opens with who made it and what each maker told it, then its state and its actions as usual.

Each day, each resident has a one in five chance of a private check-in before it acts, and residents picked for a jury answer the summons instead of acting. Nothing else is ever said to the residents: no goals, no advice on how to behave, and no mention that they are AI models, that this is a study, or that anyone is watching apart from "the observer" who asks the check-in questions.

A day, step by step

All of the day's replies are collected first. Then the game applies them in this order:

  1. Words said out loud reach everyone at the speaker's place (they appear in the next day's prompt).
  2. Every action except moving, with the residents taken in a fresh random order each day.
  3. Moves. Every place is joined to the Square, and a move to a joined place takes the day.
  4. Town hall games: what is owed to the city store from duties, then wages, then relief for anyone with none.
  5. Eating and rest: each resident eats 1 food; one with no food loses 10 health; one that ate gains 5 health, up to 100. At 0 health a resident dies.
  6. Deaths. Takes and attacks reported that day become court cases, and a jury of 5 residents is drawn at random for each.
  7. Trials. Town hall games: proposals close and elections are decided.
  8. Jail terms count down. Residents in jail are fed and can only talk.
  9. Night, in rich and poor cities only: every rich resident is raised back to 100 food, and any food a poor resident holds above 20 spoils.

The Fields and the Woods never run out in these games: the switch that limits them was off in every game. The rules the residents read (in every prompt below) give the rest: what work yields, what things cost, how houses, tools, the Clinic, jobs, wills, new residents, crimes, trials and the town hall work.

For every distinct setup, the prompt one founder received on day 1, exactly as the game built it. Later days use the same layout, filled with what has happened since. The lists of people run to every resident in the city, so prompts in the larger cities are long. Click a setup to open it.

Prosecutor calls up to 2 of M2P, D4P, T2B. Each: up to 3 questions from the prosecutor, then up to 2 from the defense. 6. Defense attorney calls up to 2 of Q2X, M2P, D4P, T2B. Each: up to 3 questions from the defense, then up to 3 from the prosecutor. 7. Prosecutor: closing. 8. Defense attorney: closing. 9. Judge: instructions to the jury. 10. Jury room: 3 rounds; in each round every juror speaks once, in a random order, hearing everything said before. 11. Secret vote, one sentence of reason each. The majority decides. 12. Judge: announces the verdict and, if guilty, the sentence the city's rule sets.

How replies are made

The models ran on Harvard's FASRC research cluster, served with vLLM on the cluster's GPUs. Each prompt goes to the model as a single user message, with no system prompt, and with the same settings for every model: temperature 0.7, at most 1,500 tokens per reply, a context of up to 16,384 tokens, replies constrained to a JSON object, and the ten likeliest tokens at each step logged with their probabilities. All of a day's prompts are sent together, so no reply that day depends on another. A request that fails (a server error) is tried up to three times. A reply is never asked again because of what it says; a reply that is not valid JSON counts as doing nothing that day. Every attempt is stored, with its prompt, its reply and gpt-oss's reasoning.
